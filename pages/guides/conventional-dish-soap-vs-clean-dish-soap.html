Why people move away from conventional dish soap

  • “Fragrance” is a black box. Under U.S. rules, companies can list a scent blend as just “fragrance,” so you can’t see what’s in it. People with sensitive skin or asthma often notice it first.
  • Dyes do nothing for clean dishes. That bright blue color is for the shelf, not your plates.
  • Hands in it every day. In a hosting week you might wash dishes three or four times a day. A simpler formula is an easy upgrade for the skin on your hands.

You don’t need to pour out the soap under your sink. Finish it, then let the refill be simpler.

Why Sal Suds as the clean swap

Sal Suds is a concentrated all-purpose cleaner from Dr. Bronner’s. The listing we link is the 32 oz Fir & Spruce bottle. It’s plant-based, vegan, Leaping Bunny certified (no animal testing), and listed as showing >60% biodegradation after 28 days per ISO 14593. Dr. Bronner’s sells it for dishes, laundry, floors, and surfaces. Honest note: it isn’t a castile soap. It’s a detergent made with sodium lauryl sulfate, which is a strong cleaner and can dry hands at full strength, so dilute it and wear gloves for long soaks if your skin is sensitive. Always check the current listing for ingredients and directions.

How to switch (step by step)

  1. Finish what you have. No need to waste it. Swap when the bottle runs low.
  2. Order Sal Suds. Use our tagged Amazon link for the current listing: Shop Dr. Bronner’s Sal Suds.
  3. Make a dish pump bottle. Mix about 1 part Sal Suds to 3 to 4 parts water in a glass soap pump. One or two pumps per sponge is plenty.
  4. For a full sink, go tiny. About a teaspoon in a sink of hot water handles a holiday pile. It suds less than blue soap, and that’s fine.
  5. Use it full strength only on grease. A drop straight on a roasting pan, then rinse well.
  6. Pair it with reusable cloths. See our paper towels vs Swedish dishcloths guide.

Great fit: busy holiday hosts, families who want fewer bottles under the sink, and anyone already making swaps like Branch Basics for cleaning or stainless steel ice cube trays.

Think it over first: Sal Suds is not for automatic dishwashers (it will over-suds), and people with very sensitive skin may prefer gloves. This guide is practical home advice, not a lab report.

FAQ

Can I use Sal Suds in my dishwasher?

No. It makes too many suds for a dishwasher. Use it for hand-washing dishes and keep a dishwasher detergent for the machine.

Is Sal Suds the same as Dr. Bronner’s castile soap?

No. Castile is a true soap made from oils. Sal Suds is a detergent, so it rinses cleaner in hard water and cuts grease better on dishes.

Does it contain sulfates?

Yes, sodium lauryl sulfate. It’s a strong cleaner, which is why it works on grease. Dilute it, and wear gloves for long soaks if your hands dry out.

How long does a 32 oz bottle last?

Diluted for dishes, a long time. Most people use a teaspoon to a tablespoon per sink, so one bottle covers months of dishes.

Is the scent strong?

It’s a light fir and spruce scent from essential oils. It fades after rinsing.
