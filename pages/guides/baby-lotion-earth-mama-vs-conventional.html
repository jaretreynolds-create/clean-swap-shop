What’s actually in everyday “gentle” baby lotion?

Conventional baby lotion is often marketed with soft language and soft lighting — but the ingredient deck can still look like adult body lotion with a nursery label. Common concerns for daily use on thin, permeable infant skin include:

  • Parabens. Preservatives used to stop microbial growth; some families prefer to minimize them because of endocrine-disruption research and out of an abundance of caution for babies.
  • Mineral oil. A petroleum-derived emollient that sits on skin and locks in moisture — effective, but not the plant-based nourishment many parents want for daily massage.
  • PEGs (polyethylene glycols). Used as thickeners and emulsifiers; they can be contaminated with 1,4-dioxane residuals depending on manufacturing, and they add to a long synthetic list.
  • Synthetic fragrance. “Baby powder” scent is often a proprietary blend — not a single flower — and can irritate sensitive skin and airways.

None of this means every conventional bottle is “poison.” It means the default formula was optimized for shelf life, texture, and scent — not for the short, clean ingredient list many parents now seek.

FAQ

Is mineral oil really a problem in baby lotion?

Mineral oil is generally considered low-irritancy and locks in moisture well. The concern for many clean-living families is origin (petroleum) and preference for plant oils that also nourish. If mineral oil bothers your values more than your baby’s skin, a botanical lotion is an easy, tangible swap.

Will Earth Mama feel as silky as Johnson’s?

Texture differs — plant oils absorb differently than mineral-oil emulsions. Most parents find a small amount is enough; warm it between your palms and massage slowly. Silky drugstore glide is not the only measure of a good lotion.

Is it safe for newborns?

Earth Mama markets products for pregnancy and baby care with organic standards in mind. Still: patch-test, follow age guidance on the label, and ask your pediatrician if your newborn has special skin needs.

What about fragrance — do babies need scent?

No. Babies do not need perfume. If you love a light herbal note, choose thoughtfully; if scent triggers congestion or rashes in your house, seek unscented or very lightly botanical options.

Can I mix conventional and Earth Mama during transition?

Yes. Use the new lotion for daily massage while you finish the old bottle on less sensitive areas — or simply retire the old bottle when you are ready. No need to layer both on the same skin at once.
