Unwrap a new vinyl shower curtain and you know the smell. Many cheap curtains and liners are PVC (vinyl), a plastic that often needs plasticizers to stay soft, and that “new curtain” smell comes from chemicals off-gassing into a small, steamy, closed room. Then the bottom gets pink and mildewy, and it goes in the trash. A 100% cotton shower curtain is the boho bathroom swap: soft texture, machine washable, and no vinyl smell.

Why people move away from vinyl curtains

  • PVC and plasticizers. Vinyl often relies on plasticizers such as phthalates to stay flexible, and some get released over time, especially in warm, humid air.
  • Off-gassing in a closed room. That new-curtain smell comes from volatile chemicals released into a small bathroom with hot water running.
  • Disposable by design. Mildew at the hem usually means a new plastic curtain every few months.

No need to rip yours down today. When it gets mildewy or torn, let the replacement be cotton.

How to switch (step by step)

  1. Measure your tub or shower. Most standard tubs fit 72" × 72".
  2. Order the curtain. Shop the mDesign 100% Cotton Shower Curtain.
  3. Choose a liner. Skip the vinyl liner and pick a fabric one so water stays in the tub.
  4. Wash before hanging. Cold, gentle cycle, then hang to dry.
  5. Spread it out after showers. Pull the curtain open so it dries fully and doesn’t mildew.
  6. Wash monthly. Add it to a towel load, skip fabric softener, and hang to dry.

Who this swap is (and isn’t) for

Great fit: anyone replacing a mildewy curtain, renters refreshing a bathroom, and families already swapping air fresheners for diffusers.

Think it over first: a cotton curtain needs a liner to keep water in the tub and a monthly wash to stay fresh. If that’s a hard no, PEVA is a less smelly plastic step down from PVC.

FAQ

Do I need a liner with a cotton curtain?

Usually, yes. The mDesign listing recommends one. A fabric liner keeps water in the tub without bringing vinyl back.

Will cotton get moldy?

Any curtain left bunched and damp can mildew. Spread it open after showers and wash it regularly.

What about PEVA curtains?

PEVA is chlorine-free and has less smell than PVC, but it’s still plastic. Cotton is the simpler swap if you can wash it.

How do I wash it?

Follow the listing: machine wash cold on gentle, hang to dry, and warm iron if needed.

Does it fit standard hooks?
