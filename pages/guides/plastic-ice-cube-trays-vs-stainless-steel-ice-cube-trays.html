Why people move away from plastic ice trays

  • Freezer odors. Plastic and silicone can soak up smells from the freezer, and the ice tastes like it.
  • Cracking and replacing. Cold makes plastic brittle. Most trays crack within a few years and end up in the trash.
  • Plastic and food, again. If you’ve already swapped plastic food storage, the ice tray is one of the last plastic pieces holding something you eat or drink.

You don’t have to throw out a working tray today. When yours cracks or smells, make steel the next one.

How to switch (step by step)

  1. Measure your freezer shelf. These trays are about 11 inches long, so make sure they fit flat.
  2. Order the trays. Use our tagged Amazon link for the current listing: Shop the Ecozoi Stainless Steel Ice Trays.
  3. Wash before first use. Warm water and a drop of simple dish soap, then dry.
  4. Fill about three-quarters full. Water expands as it freezes, and a little room makes the lever easier.
  5. Rest, then pull. Let the tray sit on the counter about 5 minutes, then lift the lever to release the cubes.
  6. Start with clean water. Ice is only as clean as what goes in. See our tap water vs Berkey filter guide.

FAQ

Do stainless steel trays make ice faster?

The listing says yes. Metal moves cold faster than plastic, so cubes often freeze sooner.

Will my fingers stick to the metal?

They can if your hands are wet and the tray is straight from the freezer. Grab it with dry hands or a towel.

Are they really plastic-free?

The trays and lever are listed as 304 stainless steel, and the listing says the packaging is plastic-free and recycled.

Can I freeze baby food or broth in them?

Yes, the listing mentions smoothies and baby food. Let them thaw slightly before pulling the lever.

Are they dishwasher-safe?

The listing says yes. Dry them fully to keep them bright.
