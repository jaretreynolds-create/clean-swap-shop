What’s actually in synthetic shampoo?

Conventional shampoo is built to foam hard and smell like a department-store sample. Behind that experience, labels often hide a familiar trio:

  • Sulfates (SLS / SLES and cousins). Strong surfactants that lift oil fast — and can leave scalp and lengths dry, frizzy, or oddly dependent on heavy conditioners and silicones to feel soft again.
  • Parabens. Common preservatives people watch for hormone-disruption concerns. Many clean brands simply formulate without them.
  • Synthetic fragrance. “Fragrance” or “parfum” can stand in for dozens of undisclosed chemicals. That cloud of scent is not the same as a few drops of essential oil you can name.

Foam is not the same as clean. A gentler surfactant system plus a good rinse removes the day’s grit without turning your scalp into a desert that then “needs” five more products.

FAQ

Why does clean shampoo foam less?

Sulfates create that huge lather. Gentler surfactants clean with less foam. Less bubbles does not mean less clean — it usually means less strip.

Will my hair feel greasy at first?

Sometimes, especially if you are used to daily clarifying. Wash thoroughly, use a little less product, and give your scalp a week or two to rebalance oil production.

Is Innersense safe for color-treated hair?

Clean, sulfate-free washes are often preferred for color longevity. Still check the specific Innersense product page for your color routine, and patch-test if your scalp is reactive.

Are sulfates and parabens definitely “toxic”?

We stay research-minded, not alarmist. Sulfates are effective detergents that can irritate; parabens are preservatives many families prefer to skip. This is a practical preference swap — cleaner labels, calmer bathrooms — not a medical diagnosis.

Do I need to toss conditioner and styling products too?

Start with shampoo — it is the highest-contact wash step. Then audit conditioners and stylers for the same fragrance and silicone stack if you want a full cabinet clean-out.
