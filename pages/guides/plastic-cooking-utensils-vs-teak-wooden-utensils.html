Look in the crock next to your stove. For most of us it holds a few black plastic spatulas, a nylon slotted spoon with a melted edge, and maybe a silicone scraper or two. They’re cheap and they don’t scratch nonstick pans, but they also spend their whole lives in hot oil, simmering soup, and sizzling skillets. Plastic and heat aren’t a great pairing, and a melted tip is plastic that went somewhere. Fall is soup, chili, and sauté season. A teak utensil set is the boho kitchen swap: warm wood in the pan, gentle on cookware, and one more piece of plastic out of the hot zone.

Why people move away from plastic utensils

  • Plastic in the hottest spot in the kitchen. Spatulas and spoons sit in hot oil and simmering pots. European food-safety agencies have advised keeping hot contact time short for nylon (polyamide) utensils because they can release small plastic compounds at high temperatures.
  • Black plastic questions. A widely shared 2024 study found flame retardants in some black plastic kitchen utensils, likely from recycled electronics plastic. The authors later corrected a math error in their exposure estimate, but the finding that those chemicals showed up in kitchenware still stood.
  • Melted, warped, replaced. A tip that softened on the pan edge means a new set in the cart every year or two.

You don’t have to empty the crock tonight. Swap the spatula and spoon you reach for most, then let the rest go as they wear out.

How to switch (step by step)

  1. Audit the crock. Pull out anything with a melted, cracked, or flaking edge first. Those are the easiest to let go.
  2. Order the set. Use our tagged Amazon link for the current listing: Shop the Zulay Kitchen Teak Utensil Set.
  3. Wash before first use. Warm soapy water, rinse, and dry completely.
  4. Hand wash, never soak. Wash soon after cooking, towel dry, and let them air out in the crock or hang them by the handle holes. Keep them out of the dishwasher.
  5. Refresh when dry. If the wood ever looks pale or rough, a light rub of food-grade mineral oil or board cream brings it back.
  6. Pair it with your board. Teak utensils plus a teak cutting board takes plastic out of most of your daily prep.

FAQ

Are wooden utensils safe for nonstick pans?

Yes. Wood is softer than metal and won’t scratch nonstick coatings, which is one of the main reasons people choose it.

What about silicone instead?

Food-grade silicone handles heat far better than black plastic or nylon and is a reasonable middle ground. We like teak because it’s plastic-free, long-lasting, and looks beautiful on the counter.

Can they go in the dishwasher?

No. Long soaks and dishwasher heat can crack or warp wood. Hand wash, dry fully, and store them upright or hanging.

Is the finish food-safe?

The listing describes a clear lacquer finish to keep the wood smooth and glossy. Check the current listing and the brand’s care notes for details on the exact set you buy.

Are the handles long?

Some reviewers mention the handles are on the shorter side. If you cook in deep stockpots a lot, keep one long-handled spoon or ladle in the crock too.
