Why people move away from parchment and foil

  • Holiday kitchens go through liners fast. Two trays of vegetables, a pan of dressing, cookies for the porch — that’s a lot of paper and foil in the trash by Friday.
  • Foil is a one-use metal sheet against food. It tears, it sticks, and acidic or salty dishes are a poor match for bare aluminum.
  • Some greaseproof papers raise extra questions. Certain stain- or grease-resistant papers have historically used PFAS treatments. A reusable mat lets you skip the paper aisle. Always read current packaging; this is practical home advice, not a lab test of every brand.

You don’t need to toss the roll in the drawer tonight. Finish it, then let the next tray be reusable.

FAQ

Do I still need parchment or foil?

For most roasting and cookies, no. Very wet candy work, lining a pan with tall sides, or tightly wrapping leftovers may still call for paper, foil, or another tool. Use the mat for everyday trays.

Can I put the mat directly on the oven rack?

No. The listing says to use it with a baking tray for support and not to place it directly on the oven rack.

Will it melt?

Not if you stay at or under 480°F, the limit on the current listing. Keep it away from a broiler or any setting hotter than that.
