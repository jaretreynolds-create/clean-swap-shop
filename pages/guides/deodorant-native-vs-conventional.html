What’s actually in conventional antiperspirant?

Drugstore antiperspirants are built around aluminum salts (often aluminum chlorohydrate or aluminum zirconium compounds). They reduce wetness by forming a temporary plug in the sweat duct. Alongside that active, many sticks add:

  • Aluminum compounds. The wetness-control engine. Research on long-term absorption and hormone signaling is ongoing and often debated — enough that many families simply prefer not to apply aluminum to thin underarm skin every morning.
  • Propylene glycol. A common solvent and texture helper that can irritate sensitive skin, especially when you shave.
  • Triclosan (legacy / some formulas). An antimicrobial once common in personal care; regulators and brands have moved away from it in many products, but it remains a toxin people still watch for on older labels.
  • Synthetic fragrance. “Fragrance” can hide dozens of chemicals. That “powder fresh” scent is a blend sitting against warm skin all day.

Sweat itself is not the enemy — odor is mostly bacteria feeding on sweat on the skin surface. Antiperspirant solves wetness; deodorant solves smell. Knowing which problem you actually care about makes the swap feel less like a sacrifice.

It does not claim to stop sweat the way antiperspirant does — and that honesty is part of why it works for people who want a toxin→clean swap without medical theater.

Families and individuals who make this swap usually care about:

Will your underarms feel identical to antiperspirant on day one? Probably not. Many people notice a short “detox” or microbiome adjustment week where odor shifts before it settles. Plan for that — it is normal, not a failure of the product.

FAQ

Does Native stop sweating like Secret?

No — and that is intentional. Antiperspirant blocks sweat; deodorant manages odor. You may still sweat (healthy), but many people find odor stays manageable once they finish the transition week.

Why does natural deodorant smell worse at first?

Your underarm microbiome and leftover antiperspirant residue are adjusting. Wash gently, stay consistent for one to two weeks, and consider a sensitive formula if irritation shows up.

What if baking soda irritates my skin?

Choose Native’s baking-soda-free / sensitive options. Redness after shaving is a common reason people bounce off early natural sticks — the sensitive line exists for that.

Is aluminum definitely “toxic”?

We stay research-minded, not alarmist. Aluminum salts are effective at stopping wetness; questions about long-term dermal exposure are why many families prefer an aluminum-free routine for daily use. This is a preference swap grounded in caution, not a diagnosis.

Will it stain shirts?

Any deodorant can mark fabric if you over-apply. Use a light layer, let it set, and wash athletic clothes promptly. White marks are usually product residue, not “toxins escaping.”
