**This week’s swap:** scented paraffin candles → **Big Dipper Wax Works Pure Beeswax Tealights**.

It’s officially candle season — darker evenings, soup on the stove, a little flicker on the table. Most store-bought scented candles are made from paraffin (a petroleum-derived wax), scented with a synthetic fragrance blend, and often colored with dye. Burned for hours in a closed-up fall house, that’s a lot of “pumpkin spice” that never met a pumpkin. None of this means you’re doing autumn wrong — it just means the default candle was built for a big scent throw, not the calm cream-and-terracotta home we’re building.

**The clean swap:** pure beeswax candles — one natural wax, a cotton wick, no added fragrance, and that warm golden glow. Think terracotta holder, linen napkin, a pot of cinnamon and orange peel simmering if you want scent. You’re not tossing your candle shelf tonight. Burn what you love, then let the next restock be beeswax.

**Toxin bite (keep it simple):** Paraffin is petroleum-based; “fragrance” on a candle label is usually an undisclosed blend; dyes add one more thing to burn; and any candle smokes when the wick runs long — trim it. Look for “100% pure beeswax” (not “made with beeswax”) when you’re ready to swap.
