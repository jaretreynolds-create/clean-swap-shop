Fresh can mean *actually clean*, not chemically loud. Swap the load you’re already doing — towels and sheets first.

## Day 4 — Kitchenware: Nonstick / PFAS → Cast iron

**Hook**  
Scratched nonstick isn’t just ugly — it’s a forever-chemical story.

**Full caption**  
Nonstick pans sell effortless eggs. Traditional PTFE coatings sit in the broader PFAS family — “forever chemicals” that stick around in the environment and raise questions when pans overheat or the coating flakes. Lodge cast iron is the opposite bet: heavier, skill-rewarding, and built to last once you learn a simple seasoning rhythm. Start with one skillet. Season with a thin oil layer, heat, cool. Eggs get better with practice; pancakes forgive you. You’re not tossing every pan tonight — retire the worst scratched ones first and cook the meals you make most. A pan that ages with you beats a coating you replace every few years. Practical kitchen swap, not a Pinterest museum.

## Day 6 — Home: Air fresheners → Essential oil diffuser

**Hook**  
If the air “smells clean,” check *what* you’re breathing.

**Full caption**  
Plug-ins and aerosol “fresheners” often mask odor with synthetic fragrance — and that fragrance can include phthalates and other VOCs that linger in indoor air long after the spray settles. Especially tough in small apartments, nurseries, and rooms with poor ventilation. A calmer swap: open a window when you can, clean the source of the smell, then diffuse a simple essential oil blend you actually like (citrus for kitchens, lavender or cedar for evenings). An essential oil diffuser won’t chemically “erase” trash-day reality — and that’s the point. Real clean smells quieter. Start with one room you live in most. Your lungs will notice before your feed does.
