**This week's swap:** plastic electric kettle → **Cosori 1.7L Borosilicate Glass Electric Kettle**.

The last two weeks we swapped the cutting board and the spoon in the pot. This week it's the appliance you probably use most: the kettle. Coffee, tea, oatmeal, a hot water bottle on a chilly night. Many budget kettles are plastic inside, with plastic walls, plastic filters, and a plastic water window, so boiling water sits against plastic every day. If yours ever had that "new plastic" taste, you already know the feeling. None of this means you did anything wrong. Those kettles were built to be cheap, not to be the cleanest thing on the counter.

**The clean swap:** a glass kettle. The Cosori kettle we link has a borosilicate glass body with a stainless steel filter and heating plate, so the water sits in glass and steel. It boils in about 3 to 7 minutes, shuts itself off, has boil-dry protection, and the wide mouth makes descaling with lemon or vinegar easy. Our link is the Ivory White version. Honest note: the handle and base are still plastic, but the water isn't touching them. You don't have to toss a working kettle tonight. When yours starts to stain or taste off, make glass the next one.

**Toxin bite (keep it simple):** Heat is what makes plastic shed the most. A 2020 *Nature Food* study found that polypropylene baby bottles released microplastic particles when filled with hot water, and that hotter water meant more. Kettles weren't tested, but it's the same pairing: plastic plus very hot water. Glass sidesteps it.
