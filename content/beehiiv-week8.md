Hi friend,

**This week's swap:** plastic cutting board → **Teakhaus Edge-Grain Teak Wood Cutting Board**.

It's full-on prep season: butternut squash, apples for crisp, onions for every soup, and a holiday bird on the horizon. Most of that chopping happens on a plastic board. Look closely at yours and you'll see the knife grooves. Each groove is plastic that went somewhere, and researchers have found that chopping on plastic boards can shed microplastic particles into food. None of this means your kitchen is doing it wrong. The plastic board was just built to be cheap and dishwasher-friendly, not to last.

**The clean swap:** a solid teak board. Teak is dense and naturally oily, so it resists moisture and warping, is kind to knives, and only needs a hand wash and a monthly oiling. Warm honey-brown wood on a cream counter, and it doubles as the cheese board when friends come over. You don't have to toss your plastic board tonight. Plenty of families keep it for raw meat and let wood handle everything else.

**Toxin bite (keep it simple):** Most plastic boards are polyethylene or polypropylene. Deep knife scars mean shedding, and they're harder to clean, which is why worn plastic boards are meant to be replaced. One good wood board can replace years of plastic ones.
